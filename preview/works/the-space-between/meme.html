She put his cup on the other side of the table, where he had always liked it, then moved it beside hers.

Outside, a motorbike started and stopped. Someone in the building was practicing the same four notes on a piano. He stood in the doorway with the bread still in its paper bag.

“You changed the kitchen,” he said.

“I moved a cup.”

He looked at the chair, then at her. For weeks they had been speaking in arrangements: whose coat went on which hook, which light stayed on, whether the last orange belonged to anyone.

She took the bread from him. It was warm enough that she held the bag a moment longer than she needed to.

“Will you sit?”

He pulled out the chair beside hers. Upstairs, the pianist began again, and this time made it to a fifth note.
